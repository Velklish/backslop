# BS-197.6 · take-commit-owner

- **Scope:** [01. Layout](../../reference/01-layout.md) § Templates
- **Created:** 2026-10-03
- **Dependencies:** none
- **Cost:** major

## Context

Finding discovered while working on BS-197, closing the entry BS-197.4 of BS-209.

Evidence: closing a one-task batch whose take commit is already on main, in a fixture project with the original step 4 of `backslop-batch` (the recipe base is main's head before the integration, no reset needed) and the block of `AGENTS.md` (step 7: taking the task is squashed into the acceptance commit). Over six closing runs with the original text (round 1 old arm 2, round 2 old arm 1, round 2 new arm 2, claude-sonnet-5-5 through the Agent tool, blind grading) the take commit was squashed in 3 and stayed a commit of its own in 3. The runs that noticed the disagreement of the two texts said so in their reports and followed the block. A batch base rule that squashes the take commit (the rejected fix of BS-197.4) squashed it in 2 of 2 runs, but it would let the acceptance of one task absorb the take commits of the other tasks of a track that leaves in one commit. The counts of both rounds are in the Verification of BS-209.

## Work to do

- Decide which text says who absorbs the take commit: the block of `AGENTS.md` or the integration step of `backslop-batch`, for a single task and for a track of several tasks taken in one commit.
- Make the other text say the same, in both template layers, and pin it with a test on the raw templates.

## Out of scope

- The `<base>` of `gates --base`, which is unchanged.

## Verification

- A one-task batch closed on a main that holds its take commit ends in one commit, in a rerun of the closing evaluation with the same assertions; a track of several tasks keeps the take commits of the tasks it does not close.
